Overview & context

Accurate tracking is not enough; the control chain must own failure.

Outdoor cone courses bring lighting changes, shadows, occlusion, single boundaries, camera interruption, and chassis protocol differences.

Problem

Continuing to drive after target or camera loss turns a perception problem into a safety problem. A single loop mixing perception, control, mission, and ROS communication is also difficult to test and tune.

SAFETY RULEEnter safe_stop and do not auto-recover

After confirming the area and camera are safe, a service call must explicitly reset the fault.

HARDWARE RULESoftware e-stop does not replace hardware e-stop

The motor controller still needs an independent disconnect.

Architecture

A single authority from image input to safe command output.

Each layer owns one core responsibility; parameters live in YAML.

01 · IMAGECamera Topic

Receive images and monitor startup/runtime timeouts.

02 · PERCEPTIONCone Boundaries

Segment colors, filter contours, estimate center and confidence.

03 · CONTROLPID & Limits

Compute steering, limit speed, and handle lost targets.

04 · MISSIONState Machine

Mapping, red light, navigation, parking, and safety states.

05 · OUTPUTTwist / PWM

Publish chassis commands and debug topics from one point.

Technical decisions

Remove stale state and competing command writers before chasing speed.

01

Confidence with perception

Dual- and single-boundary estimates use different rules and temporal smoothing.

02

Single command authority

Steering, speed, mission, and safety state are combined at one output.

03

Explicit adapter semantics

Standard Twist and legacy PWM modes remain clearly distinct.

04

Edge-triggered mission events

Lights, laps, parking, and e-stop events avoid repeated transitions.
